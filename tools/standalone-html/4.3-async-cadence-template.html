The Async
Cadence Template

Remote work hands you one of two things. Either the flexibility to outperform every AE or SDR stuck in back-to-back meetings, or enough unstructured rope to quietly hang your own pipeline by quarter-end. The difference is never effort — it's whether your calendar reads as intentional or reactive. This is the cadence that keeps you in the first group. Fill it in, print it, and keep it where you can see it.

II The Weekly Cadence Stay visible, stay deliberate

Out of sight, out of mind, out of a job. Async only works when your output is visible to your manager every week and your calendar reads as intentional. Two rituals carry the week: the Friday close-out and the Monday set-up.

Friday — audit the week you just ran. Go meeting by meeting and write a one-word verdict on each: did it advance a deal or improve your close rate? Total the hours lost to the ones that didn't. Those are your decline candidates for next week. Last week's hours lost to no-impact meetings:
Friday — send the weekly async update. A short written summary of what you shipped and what's next, sent to your manager. This is what replaces three status meetings and keeps you visible without being live in a room you don't need to be in. My manager update goes out every:
Monday — build next week's blocks before the calendar fills itself. Lay down your deep-work and selling blocks first, then let everything else fit around them. Share your "no-meeting zone" with your manager so the protection is on the record.
Weekly — protect one 1:1 worth keeping. The coaching session where you bring a real call recording or sequence and get specific feedback is your growth engine. That one stays. The recurring sync with no agenda does not.
Track your async ratio Once a week, work out the split: what share of your hours went to synchronous meetings versus asynchronous deep work? Write last week's number and set next week's target. The number going up over the month is the proof your calendar is getting more intentional.

III The Monthly Cadence The longer horizon

Weeks keep your pipeline moving; months are where the structure and the skills compound. Once a month, step back from the dials and look at the shape of how you're working.

Run a full calendar audit. Look across the whole month, not one week. Which recurring meetings still earn their slot, and which have quietly become a standing slot you keep attending out of habit? Decline or downgrade the ones that no longer move your numbers.
Renegotiate one recurring meeting into an async update. Every month, move at least one standing meeting off your calendar and onto a written or Loom update. Recurring meetings are the slow leak; this is how you patch one each month.
Spend 30 minutes on a skill that compounds outside your quota. One post breaking down a cold-email framework, one coaching outline, one Loom teardown. The skills you've built in sales have market value — the monthly habit is testing that, not just talking about it. This month I'm testing:
Check the trend, not the day. Is your async ratio climbing? Is your visible weekly output holding up? A single bad week is noise. A month of reactive calendars is a pattern — and patterns are what get noticed at review time.
IV Notification Triage What earns an interrupt

Responding to every Slack notification as it arrives is the most expensive busywork there is — it shreds the focus your deep-work block depends on. The rule is simple: almost nothing earns the right to interrupt you live. Batch the rest.

Channel / signalThe moveVerdict
Phone push notifications (Slack, email) Turn them off. If the building is on fire, they'll call you. Nothing else needs your phone buzzing during a call block. Mute
Slack during your deep-work block Closed. Set your status to focus, batch-check it on the way out of the block — not the moment a dot appears. Mute
Non-essential / company-wide channels Mute the channel outright, or check once a day. None of it advances a deal in real time. Mute
Direct message from your AE/SDR counterpart or manager Keep, but answer in batches at the block boundaries — not instantly. Set the expectation that async means a reply within hours, not seconds. Keep
Active-deal channel or prospect reply Keep. A live prospect reply is the one signal that legitimately earns the interrupt — that's revenue moving. Keep
"Optional" all-hands / no-agenda sync Decline live. Watch the recording at 1.5x on your own time, or read the notes. Never live during selling hours. Mute
When you decline, decline with tact Use a script so it lands as discipline, not avoidance:
No agenda "Is there an agenda? I've got demos / a call block stacked this week — happy to send a Loom update instead."
Recurring, no deal value "I want to protect my pipeline / call block — can we move this to a weekly async update instead?"
Protect your time like it's pipeline — because every protected hour is a deal you actually get to advance.

Your close rate lives in the structure of your calendar, not in your effort level. This template is the framework that keeps that structure from drifting, week after week. Nobody cares where you work. They care what you ship — so make the cadence visible, and make it daily.
