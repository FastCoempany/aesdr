The Manager Archetype Map

Most managers run on one operating system under pressure. Read which one yours defaults to, then use the matching weekly move to manage up on purpose — before their assumptions about you write your review for you.

How to use this
Find the archetype your manager runs on this quarter — most people are one type with a second showing up under stress. Read what that type reads as competence and what it reads as a threat. Then run the weekly move for that type, every week, in writing. You are not changing your manager. You are removing the gaps they would otherwise fill with their own guesses.
The four operating systems
01
Archetype 01
The Coach
Asks. Listens. Mirrors you back to yourself.
Reads as competence
  • You’ve thought about it.
  • You can name the gap.
  • You came with the question, not the complaint.
Reads as threat
  • You ask them to decide for you.
  • You wait to be told.
Your weekly move

End your 1:1 with one decision you made this week, one you’re stuck on, and the option you’re leaning toward.

02
Archetype 02
The Operator
Owns the dashboard. The number is the conversation.
Reads as competence
  • Pipeline math that reconciles.
  • Forecasts that don’t drift.
  • Activity tied to a specific stage gap.
Reads as threat
  • Vibes-based updates.
  • “Working on it.”
  • Numbers that move without a story.
Your weekly move

Send a Friday five-line: last week’s number, this week’s, the gap, what’s closing it, what isn’t.

03
Archetype 03
The Closer
Wants in the room. Reads silence as you hiding the deal.
Reads as competence
  • You invite them in early.
  • You tell them the risk before they ask.
  • You let them play the role they’re best at.
Reads as threat
  • Surprise losses.
  • Calls they weren’t on.
  • Deals that stall in their blind spot.
Your weekly move

Pre-brief them on one live deal mid-week. Tell them the part you’re not sure about. Let them decide if they’re joining.

04
Archetype 04
The Ghost
Skips. Reschedules. Surfaces only when something’s wrong.
Reads as competence
  • No surprises.
  • Self-managed pipeline.
  • Async, written updates they can scan.
Reads as threat
  • Surfacing problems they have to solve.
  • Pulling them into meetings.
  • Anything that needs them present.
Your weekly move

Write your update. Don’t ask for their time. Make the next step a yes/no question they can answer in 20 seconds.

Three more you’ll meet on the SDR floor
05
Archetype 05
The Metric Maniac
Counts the dials. Never asks what the dials produced.
Reads as competence
  • Activity numbers tied to an outcome.
  • A reason for any dip, before they spot it.
  • Raw counts presented with the insight next to them.
Reads as threat
  • A 5% drop in activity with no explanation.
  • Quiet weeks they can’t read.
  • Output they have to ask twice about.
Your weekly move

Report the number with the reframe attached: “Dials are down eight, connects are up fifteen percent because I qualified faster.” Never let a metric travel without its narrative.

06
Archetype 06
The Template Tyrant
One script for everyone. Deviation reads as defiance.
Reads as competence
  • You run the template first, then earn the edits.
  • Changes backed by reply rates, not opinions.
  • You frame your tweak as a test, not a rebellion.
Reads as threat
  • Going off-script with nothing to show for it.
  • “I just thought this sounded better.”
  • Looking like you think the system is beneath you.
Your weekly move

Run the template, then run one personalized variant beside it and track both. Bring the response-rate comparison: “Standard pulled 4%, the personalized line pulled 11% — can I keep testing it?” Let the data make your case.

07
Archetype 07
The Two-Faced Cheerleader
Praises you in the room. Takes it apart in the 1:1.
Reads as competence
  • You ask for the real feedback directly.
  • You keep a written record of what was agreed.
  • You close the gap between the public line and the private one.
Reads as threat
  • Being held to the public praise in front of others.
  • Feedback they can’t walk back later.
  • You naming the contradiction out loud.
Your weekly move

After every 1:1, send a short written recap of what they said and what you agreed to fix. Ask one specific question: “What’s the one thing you’d change before next week?” The record keeps the feedback consistent.

You are not trying to change your manager. You are removing the gaps they fill with assumptions about you.
