The CRM
Survival Guide

Your CRM is the record that proves you did the work this quarter — or the case that gets built against you in a QBR. The forecast leadership reads is the one you typed. When it's wrong, the gap lands on you, not on the platform.

This guide keeps the CRM the first thing — the record that protects you — without it eating 40% of your week. The fixes are specific. Name the view, name the macro, do the work.

§ 01 — The five things that make it hurt

The Pain Points, and What Actually Fixes Them

CRM feels like it was built to slow you down. It wasn't. Most of the friction is poor setup and lazy habits, not the software. Each pain point below has a fix you can do this week — none of them is "ask IT."

15 clicks → 1 macro
1
Time Suck — Click Chaos
Logging one call takes fifteen clicks

"It takes 15 clicks just to log a single call." So you batch it for Friday, then forget half of it.

Fix The CRM isn't slow — your setup is. Build a Quick Action (disposition macro) that logs the call, sets the next step, and stamps the date in one click. Pin the tabs you open all day so you stop hunting through the menu.

Dirty Data — Garbage In / Out
Duplicate accounts and missing emails

"My pipeline is full of duplicate accounts and missing emails." Two of you are working the same prospect and neither of you knows it.

Fix This is user error, not a broken platform. Run a dedupe pass (DemandTools or the native dupe rules), then turn on mandatory fields so an incomplete record can't be saved in the first place. Garbage out always traces back to garbage in.

Bad Reports — Useless Metrics
A dashboard full of charts you don't read

"Our dashboard is a mess of charts I don't understand." So you ignore it, which means you find out a deal stalled when your manager does.

Fix Strip it to what moves the number: pipeline by stage, conversion rate, deals with a close date in the past. Drop the vanity metrics — average call duration tells you nothing. A dashboard you actually read is one with three widgets, not thirty.

Automation — Over-Engineered Rules
Fifty task reminders a day for dead leads

"I get 50 automated task reminders a day for old leads." So you mark them all complete without reading — and miss the one that mattered.

Fix Automation isn't out to get you; someone over-built the rules. Audit the workflows feeding your task list, simplify the triggers, and switch off alerts firing on stages no longer in play. Fewer, sharper reminders beat a wall of noise.

5
No Discipline — Fictional Pipeline
Stages and close dates that don't reflect reality

An opportunity sits in Negotiation with no proposal sent, a close date three weeks in the past, and a next step of "follow up." The forecast you carry into QBR is fiction.

Fix Every opportunity gets an accurate stage, a real close date, and a specific next step with an owner and a date. Vague entries wreck forecast accuracy and your credibility in the pipeline review. Clean data is how you spot a stalled deal early instead of explaining it after the fact.

"CRM isn't out to get you — it's reflecting your habits back at you."
The CRM is here to stay regardless of how the team feels about it. You can complain about it for the next four quarters, or you can own it. Own the record and you own the forecast — and you stop getting blindsided in the room where it counts.
§ 02 — The under-ten-minutes-a-day rhythm

Daily CRM Discipline

A clean CRM is not a Friday project. It's a few minutes a day so it never becomes a four-hour clean-up you dread. Log in the moment, not in a batch — the call you log from memory on Friday is already half wrong. Work this list and you keep the record honest for under ten minutes a day.

Log every call before the next one. Use your Quick Action. What was discussed, the specific pain point, the next step. Never just "left voicemail."
~2 min
Update the next step on any deal you touched. A real action, a date, an owner. If you can't name the next step, the deal isn't where you think it is.
~2 min
Move anything that told you no to Closed Lost. Don't let dead deals sit in the pipeline inflating your number. Clean it the day it dies.
~1 min
Fix any close date that's already in the past. A close date you've blown through is a lie your forecast is telling for you. Reset it or close the deal.
~2 min
Work your prioritized view, not the full list. Open the saved view sorted by last activity or priority. Don't scroll the whole table looking for who to call.
~1 min
Once a week, block fifteen minutes for cleanup. Same day, same time. Dedupe, fix stale stages, complete any record missing a field. Fifteen minutes weekly beats four hours quarterly.

§ 03 — Set it up once, save the hours forever

The Salesforce Setup

Most of the 40% you lose to CRM is paid back by an afternoon of setup. These are the concrete moves — build them once and the daily rhythm above takes minutes instead of an hour.

The moveWhat it does for you
Quick Actions Build a Log a Call action with the fields pre-set — outcome, next step, date. Turns a fifteen-click chore into one click. This is the single biggest hour-saver in the list.
Disposition macros One macro per common call outcome (connected, no answer, not now, booked). Sets the status, logs the activity, and stamps the next step in a single action so you stop reconstructing calls from memory.
Saved list views Build the views you actually work: "My Open Deals by Close Date," "Stale — No Activity 14+ Days," "Leads to Call Today" sorted by priority. Stop scrolling the full table; open the view and go.
Pipeline dashboard Three widgets, not thirty: deals by stage, close dates this quarter, weighted pipeline. See deal health at a glance and catch a stall before the pipeline review does.
Mandatory fields Make the fields that matter required — email, stage, close date, next step. An incomplete record can't be saved, so dirty data never enters in the first place.
Dedupe pass Run the native duplicate rules or a tool like DemandTools. One clean territory means you're not cold-calling an account a teammate already owns.
Workflow audit Review the automation feeding your task list. Switch off rules firing on dead stages so your reminders are few and sharp instead of fifty a day you ignore.
§ 04 — The standard to hit on every record

What a Clean Record Looks Like

When you're unsure whether a record is good enough, hold it against this. An opportunity another AE could pick up cold — or a manager could forecast off without asking you a single question — has all of it.

Stage
Accurate to where the deal actually is — not where you hope it is. Negotiation means a proposal is out.
Close date
A real date in the future, tied to the prospect's timeline. Never one already in the past.
Amount
A dollar figure you can defend — what the deal is actually worth, not a placeholder.
Next step
A specific action, a date, and who owns it. "Send revised pricing to the VP of Ops by the 14th," not "follow up."
Last activity
A logged call or email with what was discussed and the prospect's specific pain point — enough for a cold handoff.
Contact + decision-maker
Complete contact info, and the decision-maker confirmed. No missing emails, no guessing who signs.
Clean data is the cheapest insurance you'll ever buy.

Spend the few minutes a day and the CRM becomes the thing that proves your quarter. Skip it, and it becomes the case against you — built in your own handwriting. Course 9.1 was about choosing the first one on purpose. Keep this next to your keyboard and work it daily.
