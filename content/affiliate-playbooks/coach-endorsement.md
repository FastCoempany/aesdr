# Coach / 1:1 endorsement

Person-by-person referrals to current coaching clients. Lowest volume in the program. Highest conversion per send. The path where the affiliate-fit canon "borrowed trust = merciless mirror" is most exposed — and most rewarded.

## Who this is for

- Active 1:1 sales coach with current SDR/AE clients
- Fractional VP of Sales running ride-alongs and 1:1s
- Manager-coach inside a company who works the role with their reports
- Group-coaching practitioner running cohort 1:1s

The common thread: you're regularly in the room with someone working through their actual job — and they trust you enough that a recommendation registers as advice, not promotion.

## How the path works

You don't run a campaign. You don't blast a list. You mention AESDR to the specific clients who'd benefit, person-by-person, in the moments where it's contextually relevant. Then you send each of them a one-to-one note with your tracking link.

You won't send more than 8–15 of these a month even at high client volume. That's the point. The leverage is per-send conversion, not volume.

## Quick wins, first 14 days

1. **List the clients.** Open your client list. Mark the 3–5 names who'd genuinely benefit from AESDR right now — first-year SDRs hitting their first slump, AEs in a new vertical, anyone you've quietly thought *"they need someone besides me telling them what good looks like."*
2. **Send the first note.** Use template 04a from the [promo-copy-pack](/affiliates/kit-private/promo-copy-pack) — it's the 1:1 referral template. ~120 words. Personalize the opener and the "why I think this fits you" line; leave the disclosure as-is.
3. **Verbal mention in the next session.** Don't introduce it as a separate agenda item. Wait for the moment where it's actually relevant: client raises a question about cold calling, pipeline math, manager 1:1s. Then: *"There's a 6-week thing AESDR runs that's actually built for this — I send a few clients through it a quarter. Want me to forward the link?"*

The verbal-then-email sequence converts much higher than email alone. The email is the documentation; the conversation is the recommendation.

## What the first asset is

The one-to-one referral message. Template 04a. ~120 words. One person at a time, never CC'd, never BCC'd.

If you'd send the message to three clients today and the body would read the same for all three — pause. The path only works if every send is specific to the recipient. Otherwise you're running a small newsletter, and you should be on the [Newsletter feature](/affiliates/dashboard/playbooks/newsletter-feature) playbook instead.

## What to measure

| Stage | Baseline | What "good" looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Send-to-click rate | 60–80% | If a client doesn't click within a week, follow up once verbally. If they don't click after the verbal, drop it. |
| Click-to-enroll rate | 50–70% | Warm 1:1 referrals are the highest-converting traffic AESDR sees. Below 40% = the curriculum-fit was wrong for the client; rethink before you send the next one. |
| Long-tail referrals from one client to peers in their org | Untracked but real | A client who finishes AESDR and recommends it to teammates is the strongest signal you can get. Anecdotal but worth noting. |
| Refund rate, 30-day | Below 8% typical | Above 12% = you're sending to clients the program isn't built for. |

Dashboard shows the numbers per-link. Most coaches generate one tracking link and reuse it — that's fine for tracking.

## Cadence

**As-it-comes-up.** Forcing a recommendation wrecks the trust-leverage that makes the path work. If a quarter goes by where you don't have a single client where it'd fit, don't send anything. The clients you'd benefit are the ones whose work intersects what AESDR teaches; if they're not in your roster right now, they're not.

The natural cap is 8–15 sends a month at high client volume, 2–5 at normal volume. Above 20 means you're broadcasting, not recommending — and your clients can tell.

## FTC and disclosure

Two places, both straightforward:

1. **In the written 1:1 note.** Plain English: *"Quick note — I earn a small commission if you enroll. Doesn't change the price for you. I'm sending this because I think it'll help, not because of the commission."* Template 04a has this line; don't strip it.
2. **In the verbal mention.** ~5 seconds: *"Full disclosure — I'm an affiliate, so I earn if you enroll. Doesn't change anything for you."* Then continue. The client will appreciate the candor more than they'll appreciate the omission.

If a client asks whether the commission is influencing your recommendation, the honest answer is *"AESDR is the only sales curriculum I'm an affiliate for, and I'd recommend it whether they paid me or not."* If that isn't true for you, don't run this path.
