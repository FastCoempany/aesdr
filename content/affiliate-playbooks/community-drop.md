# Community drop

A pinned message in your paid community plus a one-time Q&A thread, optionally graduating to a co-promoted workshop later. Best when your community is small enough that members read the pinned channel and large enough that a 10-15% click rate produces meaningful enrollments.

## Who this is for

Paid communities of 50–2,000 sales practitioners. Circle, Discord, Slack, Substack chat, Geneva, Mighty Networks — platform doesn't matter. The path assumes:
- Members trust your curation (your pins read as recommendations, not ads), and
- You have a main / general / announcements channel with real read-through, and
- You're personally active enough that a Q&A thread would have you in it for 48 hours.

Communities above 2,000 members behave more like newsletters; if that's you, the [Newsletter feature](/affiliates/dashboard/playbooks/newsletter-feature) playbook is closer to your dynamics.

## The arc

```
Day 1     Pin the announcement in #general with link + disclosure
Day 1     Drop a one-line heads-up in your weekly digest if you have one
Day 3     Open the Q&A thread; tag a few specific members you know would benefit
Day 7-14  Q&A thread stays open; you respond personally to questions
Day 14    Optional: schedule a co-promoted workshop (graduates you to the Workshop host playbook)
Day 30    Attribution window closes; refunds settle
```

## Quick wins, first 14 days

1. **Pin the message.** Use template 06a from the [promo-copy-pack](/affiliates/kit-private/promo-copy-pack) — community-drop pinned post. 80-120 words. Disclosure baked in.
2. **Tag the right members.** In the pinned message or in the Q&A thread, @ the 3-5 members you know would benefit. Public tagging only — never DM the recommendation; it'll read as a pyramid scheme.
3. **Open the Q&A thread.** 7 days after the pin. Title it directly: *"AESDR Q&A — anything about the curriculum, the price, the refund policy, whether it fits where you are."* Stay in the thread for 48 hours.

## What the first asset is

The pinned message. Template 06a. 80-120 words. Includes the link, the disclosure, and one specific reason your community in particular would benefit (the line where you customize).

The customization line matters more than the rest of the post. Generic "you might like this" reads as low-effort. Specific *"the 4-week pipeline section is built for the cold-outbound problem half of you raised in March's Slack thread"* reads as curation.

## What to measure

| Stage | Baseline | What "good" looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Click-through from pinned message | 8-25% of monthly-active members | Below 5% = the pin got buried or the community isn't active enough for this path; rethink. |
| Q&A thread engagement | 5-15 questions over 48 hours | Below 3 = either the timing was off or your community isn't curious about the topic. |
| Enrollments / clicks | 12-20% for community traffic | Community members are pre-qualified; expect higher conversion than newsletter or social. |
| Refund rate, 30-day | Below 10% typical | Above 12% = curriculum-fit mismatch worth a co-debrief. |

Dashboard surfaces clicks and enrollments. Q&A engagement you read directly off your community platform.

## Cadence

**One pinned + one Q&A per quarter.** A second pin inside 90 days reads as ad-load even in a friendly community. A re-mention later in a different format (you ran the workshop, you have results to share, a member finished the program and wants to tell their story) works fine.

The natural graduation: after one or two community drops, you co-promote a workshop with AESDR specifically for your community. That graduates you to the [Workshop host](/affiliates/dashboard/playbooks/workshop-host) playbook — which has 3-5x the conversion of a pin, at the cost of significant coordination.

## FTC and disclosure

Three places, all template-handled or platform-native:

1. **In the pinned message.** Template 06a has the disclosure line. Don't strip it. *"I'm an AESDR affiliate — earn a commission if you enroll through the link. Price doesn't change. Pinned because I think it fits this community, not for the commission."*
2. **In the Q&A thread intro.** Repeat the disclosure as the thread opener. The Q&A reaches members who skipped the pin.
3. **In any DM you send a member asking about the program.** Same plain-English disclosure. Don't drop it because the conversation got casual.

Don't run a closed-mouth referral campaign in your community. The leverage of a community drop is built on the trust your members have in you not pulling that. Burning it for one quarter of commission isn't a trade.
