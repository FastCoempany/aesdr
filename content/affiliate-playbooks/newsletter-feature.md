# Newsletter feature

A single issue of your newsletter that features AESDR — either as the whole issue or as the recommended-thing inside a normal issue. Lowest coordination cost in the program. Re-runnable once a quarter without going stale.

## Who this is for

Anyone who already sends a newsletter their audience opens. Substack, Beehiiv, ConvertKit, Mailchimp, custom — platform doesn't matter. List size matters less than open rate: a 1,500-subscriber list at 45% open beats a 30,000-subscriber list at 12% every time.

The path works as soon as you have:
- A list of any size where open rate is above 25%, and
- A normal cadence (weekly, biweekly, monthly) your subscribers expect, and
- A history of recommending things (so an AESDR rec doesn't read as your first-ever sponsored mention).

## Two formats

**Format A — the whole issue.** One feature on AESDR: why it exists, who it's for, what it does that other things don't, your honest read. 800-1,500 words, in your normal voice. Highest conversion. Costs you one issue.

**Format B — the inline recommendation.** AESDR is one of three or four things in your normal weekly. 100-200 words. Lower conversion per send, but you don't burn an issue.

Pick A if you trust the audience to engage with a single recommendation. Pick B if your audience comes for breadth and would tune out a single-subject send.

## Quick wins, first 14 days

1. Decide format (A or B). If you can't decide, default to B — it's lower-stakes and you can graduate to A later.
2. Grab a copy template from the [promo-copy-pack](/affiliates/kit-private/promo-copy-pack). Templates 03a (Format A) and 03b (Format B) are the starting points. Edit the bracketed slots; don't strip the disclosure.
3. Schedule the send for your normal cadence slot 7–14 days out. Don't make it a special send — that telegraphs "ad" before the reader opens it.
4. Submit your edited copy through [Submit Copy](/affiliates/dashboard/submissions) for canon review. 24-business-hour turnaround.

## What the first asset is

The single feature issue (A) or the inline mention (B). Template in the promo-copy-pack. The body copy is editable; the disclosure line and the offer terms are not.

If your normal voice is significantly different from the template, write your own and submit it. The template is a starting point, not a contract. Originality reads better than a template anyone could spot.

## What to measure

| Stage | Baseline | What "good" looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Open rate (this send vs. your normal) | Within ±10% of your normal | Significantly lower = subject line missed. Significantly higher = audience was waiting for this read. |
| Click-through rate on the rec | 2–5% of opens for inline; 6–12% for full-issue | Below 1% = the body copy isn't earning the click. Worth a debrief. |
| Enrollments / clicks | 6–10% for warm referrals | The number that matters. Below 4% = audience-fit isn't there; consider whether to re-run. |
| Refund rate, 30-day | 8–12% baseline | Below 8% = strong audience match. Above 15% = co-investigate. |

Dashboard surfaces all of these once the send goes out and the attribution window closes.

## Cadence

**One feature per audience per quarter.** Sooner reads as ad-load, even on a warm list. A re-mention 90+ days later in a different format (an issue update, an interview, a "what changed since I recommended this" debrief) is fine — and often outperforms the first send because the audience has had time to consider.

Don't run B then A back-to-back. The audience reads the second one as "they're going harder on the sell." Run A then B six months later as a follow-up, or vice versa — both work.

## FTC and disclosure

Two places, both template-handled:

1. **At the top of the rec section** — the disclosure line is in every template: *"This is an affiliate link. I earn a commission if you enroll. The price you pay is the same."* Or your normal voice equivalent — just keep the three facts: affiliate relationship, commission, no price change.
2. **At the link itself** — short version inline: *"(affiliate link)"* or *"(I earn a commission)"*. The link itself is unchanged either way.

Don't bury the disclosure in a footnote, don't gray it out, don't put it after the unsubscribe link. Both the FTC and your audience can spot the difference between a real disclosure and a compliance one.
