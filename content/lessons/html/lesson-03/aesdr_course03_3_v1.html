Section 01 — The Diagnosis
You Hold 80% of the SDR's Review
The AE Is Your Unofficial Manager

You don't sign the SDR's review. Their SDR manager sets quota, runs the 1:1s, decides the promotion. But that manager builds most of their read on the SDR from a private line to you — they ask the AEs the SDR serves for the good, the bad, and the ugly, and they weight your answer heavily because you see the SDR's work daily and the manager doesn't.

So you lead the SDR informally whether you meant to or not. Not hire-and-fire — you can't promote them and you can't let them go. Influence. The report you give when the manager asks is most of what the manager has to go on.

  • 01The manager asks you five things. When they want a read on the SDR they ask about preparedness, availability, creativity and engagement, activity, and initiative — does the SDR show up ready, can the AE reach them, do they bring ideas, are they doing the work, do they move before being told. Those five dimensions are the review.
  • 02You answer from what you see daily. The manager sees the SDR in a 1:1 once a week. You see them on every deal. That's why your answer carries the weight it does — and why a fair, developmental report matters more than you think.
  • 03A bad report costs the SDR a review they're not in. Scapegoat a dead deal on "weak leads" and that version reaches the manager first. Coach the SDR and vouch for them, and you can carry a slow quarter for someone who's working.
The Reframe
"To the person who actually manages the SDR, you are the manager. That's exactly what your read becomes on the review."
Lead the SDR deliberately and the report you give back is fair and useful to both of you. Ignore them and the manager fills the gap with whatever the last dead deal made you say in passing.

Your AE is not your manager. Your SDR manager signs your reviews, sets your quota, decides your promotion. But here's what no one says out loud in onboarding: your manager builds 80%+ of their read on you from a private line to the AEs you serve — your preparedness, your availability, your creativity, your activity, your initiative, straight from the person who sees it daily. Anyone who tells you the AE relationship doesn't shape your review is either lying to you or junior enough that they haven't seen it yet. Treat the AE as your unofficial manager, because to the person who actually is, that's exactly what they are.

  • 01You're scored on five dimensions. Preparedness — do you show up to the AE's deals ready. Availability — can the AE reach you when a deal's hot. Creativity and engagement — do you bring ideas or just take orders. Activity — are you actually doing the work. Initiative — do you move before being told. The AE answers all five when the manager asks.
  • 02You can't change the AE — you can manage the partnership. The AE you serve is one of a handful of types you'll meet on the team. You don't get to pick which one, but you decide how you work with them so the private feedback line runs in your favor.
  • 03The report happens with or without you. Manage the partnership and your AE's answer to those five questions works for you. Ignore it and your AE answers anyway — from whatever they last saw you do, good or bad.
The Reframe
"You win a review you're not even in the room for — by managing the partnership with the person who is."
Name the AE type you're working with, work the partnership on the five dimensions, and the manager hears a version of you that you actually shaped — not the one a frustrated AE volunteers after a deal slips.

Section 03 — The Framework
The 3-Step Leading-the-SDR Framework
The 3-Step Partnership Framework

You influence the SDR's review whether you do this deliberately or not. These three steps make the influence fair and useful — to the SDR you lead and to the manager who comes to you for the read.

  • 01Name the partner type you are. The same five types apply to you. Are you a Mentor coaching the SDR, or a Coaster shrugging when their manager asks? A Machine with a fair bar, or one who only demands? Name it honestly — a Blamer who scapegoats the SDR for a dead deal costs you both, because the manager sees the pattern eventually.
  • 02Lead on the five dimensions. Give the SDR something to be prepared for, be reachable enough that they can be available to you, ask for their ideas instead of only handing out orders, and tell them what you need before you have to chase them. You can't develop initiative in someone you never give room to take it.
  • 03Give a report that's fair and developmental. When the manager asks, answer from the full picture — the work you didn't see as well as the call that went sideways. Name one thing the SDR does well and one specific thing to work on. That report is most of their standing; treat it like it is.
The Accountability Check
"You don't sign the review. You write most of it anyway. Did you write it fairly?"
A Mentor's SDRs get promoted, and that reflects on you. A Coaster's SDRs get a shrug, and the pipeline pays for it. The report you give back is the lever — use it deliberately.

You can't change which AE type you're working with. You can change how you work the partnership so the five dimensions land in your favor. These three steps run as a sequence.

  • 01Name the AE type you're working with. Mentor, Ghost, Blamer, Machine, or Coaster — write down which one each AE you serve defaults to, and the specific behavior that tells you. The move for a Ghost (build the paper trail yourself) is the opposite of the move for a Machine (match their bar), so you can't run the right play until you've named the type.
  • 02Make your work on the five dimensions visible. Show up to their deals prepared, stay reachable when a deal's hot, bring one idea instead of waiting for the order, log the activity, and move before you're asked. The AE can only report what they see — so make the five dimensions impossible to miss.
  • 03Build the paper trail the report runs on. Async prep docs, logged qualification criteria on every handoff, a Friday recap your manager reads directly. When the AE's report is generous you've earned it; when it isn't, you've got a record that argues back.
The Accountability Check
"Are you managing the partnership — or just hoping the AE says something nice?"
Hoping isn't a plan. If you can't name the AE's type or point to the prep doc you sent this week, the report happens without your fingerprints on it — and that's the one thing this lesson is meant to stop.

The Partnership Toolkit
Async Prep Doc — Per Deal (SDR)
DimensionWhat to Put in Front of the AE
Preparedness"Before our call: account, the contact, why I qualified them, and the one risk I see."
Availability"I'm reachable on Slack 8–6 and on my cell if a deal's hot — here's the number."
Creativity"One idea on this account: [angle]. Worth a try, or should I hold?"
Initiative"I already lined up the next two accounts so we're not waiting on me."
SDR Report Template — When the Manager Asks (AE)
DimensionHow to Answer Fairly
Preparedness"Shows up to my deals ready — here's a specific call where it mattered."
Availability + Activity"Reachable when I need them; the work behind the deals is there, including what doesn't cross your desk."
Creativity + Initiative"Brings ideas and moves before I ask — one recent example: [account]."
One development note"One thing to work on this quarter: [specific, fixable]. Here's how I'd coach it."
Handoff Note Library — Survives a "Weak Lead" Claim
SituationWhat to Log
Passing a qualified lead"Qualified on: budget confirmed, timeline Q3, decision-maker engaged. Notes attached."
AE goes quiet (the Ghost)"Async prep doc sent [date], standing 15-min Friday — logged so the effort is visible either way."
A deal dies"Here's every touchpoint and the qualification criteria from handoff — let's debrief before we assign a cause."
The Coaster won't advocate"Friday recap in the manager's channel: what closed, what I'm working, what I need — no one has to speak up for me."
Homework — This Week (AE)
  • Name which AE partner type you default to with the SDR you work with — Mentor, Ghost, Blamer, Machine, or Coaster. Be honest. Write down the behavior that proves it.
  • This week, lead on one of the five dimensions you've been ignoring: give the SDR something to prepare for, ask for one idea instead of an order, or tell them what you need before you have to chase them.
  • The next time the SDR's manager asks you for a read, answer on all five dimensions — and name one thing the SDR does well plus one specific, fixable thing to work on.
  • Self-reflection: when did you last let one bad call stand in for your whole read on an SDR? Write down what you said, what it likely cost them, and what the fair report would have been.
Homework — This Week (SDR)
  • Name the AE partner type for each AE you serve — Mentor, Ghost, Blamer, Machine, or Coaster. Write down the behavior that tells you, and the move that type calls for.
  • Send one async prep doc this week before a call, using the template above. Make your preparedness and one idea impossible for the AE to miss.
  • On your next handoff, log the qualification criteria using the Handoff Note Library — so a "weak lead" claim has a paper trail to argue against.
  • Self-reflection: on which of the five dimensions is your AE's read on you weakest right now — and what specific thing will you do this week to change it?
